# Spray Foam Chemical Exposure: Why Your GL Excludes Pollution Claims

Of all the exposures in spray foam contracting, chemical exposure is the one most likely to produce a six-figure third-party claim — and the one most likely to be **uncovered** by a standard policy.

Here's why, and what to do about it.

## The Pollution Exclusion in Plain English

Nearly every commercial general liability (GL) policy contains a **pollution exclusion**. The exact wording varies, but the effect is the same: the policy does not pay for bodily injury or property damage arising out of the **discharge, dispersal, release, or escape** of pollutants — and chemicals, including isocyanates and amine catalysts used in spray foam, are treated as pollutants.

That means the most common chemical-exposure claims in spray foam are excluded from a standard GL:

- An occupant **returns to a building too soon** after foam application and experiences respiratory symptoms
- **Isocyanate off-gassing** from improperly cured foam causes chemical sensitivity in building occupants
- A **neighboring unit or space** is contaminated during a foam application
- A client claims **personal injury** from chemical exposure during or after an application

Without contractor pollution liability (CPL), the spray foam contractor pays defense and settlement costs out of pocket.

## Sudden vs. Gradual — the Coverage Split

Pollution liability comes in two flavors, and the difference matters for spray foam:

- **Sudden and accidental** coverage pays for an abrupt release — a proportioner failure that sprays raw chemical, an accidental over-application, an immediate exposure incident.
- **Gradual / non-sudden** coverage pays for slow releases — off-gassing from foam that continues to release volatile compounds over days or weeks after application.

Most real spray foam claims combine both. An immediate occupant exposure is sudden; the continued off-gassing from under-cured foam is gradual. **Spray foam contractors usually need both**, and the cheapest CPL quotes cover only sudden.

## What Contractor Pollution Liability Actually Covers

A well-structured CPL policy for spray foam covers:

- **Third-party bodily injury** from chemical exposure during or after application
- **Property damage** from contamination or off-gassing
- **Defense costs** for pollution-related claims and lawsuits
- **Both sudden and gradual** releases from the spray foam application process
- **Isocyanate, amine catalyst, and blowing-agent exposure** claims

## Your Safety Practices Are Your Best Friend

The same practices OSHA and EPA want to see are exactly what underwriters want to see. Documented **re-entry protocols**, ventilation procedures, PPE requirements for occupants, and post-application air testing don't just reduce your real-world risk — they materially improve both the **placement** and the **price** of CPL coverage.
